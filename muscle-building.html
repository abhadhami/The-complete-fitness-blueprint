06 · Strength & weight-training principles

Train with a reason.

Progressive overload

Progress can come from more reps, more load, additional productive work, improved ROM or better execution—not only adding weight every session.

Volume & frequency

Use enough productive training to stimulate growth, distribute it across the week and adjust it to the person.

Effort

Use RIR/RPE to understand how hard a set is. Failure is a tool, not a requirement for every set.

Exercise order

Place important movements where the client can perform them with good quality and sufficient effort.

Rest periods

Rest long enough to maintain useful performance, especially on demanding compound movements.

Specificity

Train the qualities and movements that match the goal instead of changing exercises randomly.

10 · Pain & exercise modification

Know when to modify—and when to stop.

Shoulder, knee, back, neck, wrist or elbow discomfort

Review technique, load, ROM and exercise choice. Use a suitable regression or alternative when appropriate.

Normal soreness vs concerning pain

Teach the difference between expected training soreness, fatigue and unusual or worsening symptoms.

Simple decision system

Stop → assess → modify → re-test → refer when needed. The program does not diagnose or treat injuries.

Safety: Sharp or sudden pain, significant swelling, marked weakness/numbness, inability to use a limb normally, chest symptoms, fainting, severe breathlessness or other concerning symptoms require stopping exercise and appropriate professional/medical evaluation.

12 · Track & adjust

Performance

Body composition

Adjust
